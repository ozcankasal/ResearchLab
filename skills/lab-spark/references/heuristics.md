# Moves that make research ideas

Use several per session, and say which move produced each card. The moves are grouped by what they act on.

## On a construction you already have

- **What does it not see?** Find the invariant, kernel or measurement the construction is blind to, and the information that blindness discards. An integer invariant hidden behind a phase-insensitive quantity is a classic find.
- **Complete the picture.** If you have the modulus, ask about the phase. If you have the zero sector, ask about the others. If you have one copy, ask about two; if you have the diagonal, ask about the cross terms.
- **Generalize and specialize.** Lift to the natural symmetry group or category. Then go back down to the smallest case where something non-trivial happens.
- **Change the representation.** Fourier transform, generating function, polynomial and its zeros, Bloch sphere and Majorana stars, graph, tensor network, operator algebra. A problem often becomes easy, or obviously known, in another representation.
- **Mirror and dualize.** Apply the symmetries of the setting (conjugation, complement, time reversal, Fourier duality) and ask what is preserved and what flips.

## On a question

- **Pólya's questions.** Do you know a related problem? Can you solve a special case, an analogous one, or a more general one? Can you work backwards from the unknown? Did you use all the data and every hypothesis?
- **Look at the extremes.** Degenerate inputs, product states, maximally entangled states, n = 1, the limit of infinite size, weak and strong coupling, zero noise, total noise.
- **Invert.** Instead of computing the quantity from the state, ask what states have a given value. Instead of an upper bound, ask for a lower bound. Instead of an algorithm, ask for a hardness proof.
- **Assume it works.** Suppose the bold version is true. What would have to be true first, and is any of that already known to be false?
- **Remove or add a constraint.** Drop unitarity, purity, locality or exactness, and see what becomes possible. Add a promise, a symmetry or noise, and see what becomes provable.

## Across fields

- **Same object, other field.** Ask where else this mathematical object appears: NMR, nuclear physics, statistics, signal processing, coding theory, combinatorics, condensed matter. Steal the other field's theorems and its open problems.
- **Tool transfer.** Take a tool from one field to another's problem: model theory to quantum foundations, phase retrieval to quantum tomography, random polynomials to random states.
- **Dequantize, then requantize.** Find the classical algorithm that matches the quantum claim. The gap that remains, if any, is the real research question.

## On ideas already on the board

- **Lakatos.** Take a counterexample to a claim and decide what it means: the definition was wrong (monster-barring), a hypothesis was missing (lemma incorporation), or the claim is false. Each choice leads to a different card.
- **The outsider question.** Explain the idea to Jamie in one paragraph. Whatever needs a second paragraph is either the real content or the hidden weakness.
- **Fermi first.** Estimate the size of the effect before investing in it. A beautiful effect of size 2⁻ⁿ is a different project from one of size 1/n.
