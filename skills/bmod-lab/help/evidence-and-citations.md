# Evidence and citations

Read this when the user asks how the lab verifies claims and citations, what the citation record and the claim ledger are, or why a citation is "METADATA-ONLY".

## Why the lab is strict about this

- **Citers rarely read the original.** From the way misprints spread through reference lists, Simkin and Roychowdhury estimate that only about one citer in five has read the paper they cite.
- **Quotations are often wrong.** In medical journals about a quarter of quotations contain errors, and about one in eight is seriously wrong.
- **Language models invent references.** One study found that GPT-3.5 fabricated 55% of the references it produced and GPT-4 18%. Many of the real ones had wrong volumes, pages or dates. Every reference an AI tool suggests is UNVERIFIED until it is checked.
- **Retractions spread unnoticed.** Only about 5% of citations written after a retraction mention it.
- **A field may already have the result under another name.** The lab has met this twice already:
  - its sector harmonics turned out to be NMR's multiple-quantum intensities;
  - a winding count turned out to sit close to nuclear physics' pole rule.

## How a citation earns its level

| Level | What was done |
|---|---|
| VERIFIED | The source was opened, the passage that supports the citing sentence is quoted verbatim with its page in `references/notes/<key>.md`, and the passage says what the sentence says. |
| METADATA-ONLY | The record was checked against Crossref or arXiv (`lab-factcheck`'s `refcheck.py`), but the text was not read, for example because of a paywall. Fine for comparison-only citations; not enough for a citation a proof depends on. |
| UNVERIFIED | Not yet checked. |
| FALSE | Checked: it does not support the sentence, or it does not exist. |

Load-bearing citations are those a proof, a method or a number depends on. They must be VERIFIED. Before relying on one, also check that its hypotheses match our use, and that it has no erratum or retraction. Comparison-only citations need at least METADATA-ONLY. The paper's Sources appendix says which citations are which.

## The claim ledger

`claims.csv` in each paper project has one row per numbered statement (by LaTeX label), per brief claim (`C3`) and per quoted number (`num:<key>`). Each row gives the evidence and its level:

- FORMALIZED
- PROVED
- CHECKED
- SUPPORTED
- CONJECTURED
- UNVERIFIED

A statement typeset as a theorem or proposition needs PROVED or better. A conjecture is typeset as a conjecture. `ledger.py` checks this mechanically. The formats are in `lab-factcheck/references/citation-record.md`.

## Privacy of requests

Requests to Crossref, arXiv and other APIs carry no personal data: no names or emails in headers, queries or URLs. Crossref's "polite pool" asks for a contact address; the lab sends none, and it accepts the slower public pool and its rate limits.
